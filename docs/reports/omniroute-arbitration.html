3.3 The real strategic disagreement: timing and formality, not direction

Both audits land in the same category of the 8-option list once Codex's vocabulary is mapped (§4) — neither recommends broad adoption, replacing Ralph, or stopping either project. The actual disagreement:

  • Claude: do nothing now; wait for a concrete, proven non-CLI worker need before even a POC.
  • Codex: start a gated, reversible qualification spike now (OR-0), because the capabilities are costly to rebuild and the investigation cost is low and bounded if validation gates are respected.

Confidence: MEDIUM on which timing is better. Missing evidence in both reports: a concrete trigger event, or a build-cost estimate for just the narrow pieces we'd actually want.

6. Final arbitration

CLAUDE_RECOMMENDATION: KEEP_CURRENT_ARCHITECTURE (do nothing now; revisit on a concrete need).

CODEX_RECOMMENDATION: closest mapping ADD_OMNIROUTE_OPTIONALLY, with an immediate gated qualification spike (OR-0) rather than a deferred one.

Both reports agree on every structural question that actually decides whether either project should stop, be replaced, or be reduced (§2) — not used here as a tie-breaker, it is simply the independently-reached, evidence-backed shape of the problem: OmniRoute does not threaten ai-dev-orchestrator or Ralph, and no component should be replaced or removed today. There is no real disagreement to arbitrate on the question this audit exists to answer.

The one substantive disagreement (timing of a spike, §3.3) is resolved as follows, on the evidence presented rather than by preferring either author: ADD_OMNIROUTE_OPTIONALLY, deferred until a concrete trigger, remains the better-supported position — but the bar for "concrete trigger" should explicitly include a low-cost, bounded qualification step like Codex's OR-0 (a throwaway, isolated, non-auto, upstream-pinned instance, no production credentials, no integration into WorkerSelector). OR-0 is cheap and reversible enough not to require waiting for a "real" production need — specifically to convert today's honestly-labeled UNKNOWNs (real p50/p95 latency overhead, real compression benefit on our workload, real operational cost) into measured facts. Everything past OR-0 (actually wiring OmniRoute into WorkerSelector/ AdaptiveExecutionSelector/QuotaManager) should stay gated on a proven need, exactly as Claude's report argues and exactly as Codex's own validation gates already require.

This synthesizes the two reports into a narrower position than either stated alone: run the smallest possible OR-0-style measurement spike opportunistically, in a future session, with no code in src/orchestrator/ touched and no credentials exposed; do not commit to OR-1 or beyond without a concrete need. This is not a new 9th option — it collapses onto ADD_OMNIROUTE_OPTIONALLY in the 8-option vocabulary, with the qualification-spike detail preserved as an implementation note.

Confidence in this synthesis: MEDIUM-HIGH. High on the "no replacement, no removal, no stop" core; medium on the exact trigger threshold for even the narrow spike, because neither audit measured OmniRoute's actual overhead/benefit on our real workload — the single largest piece of missing evidence in both reports.

STOP PROJECT — arbitrated final answer

  • Can OmniRoute replace ai-dev-orchestrator? NO (both audits, HIGH confidence)
  • Can OmniRoute replace Ralph? NO (both audits, HIGH confidence)
  • Can OmniRoute replace both? NO (both audits, HIGH confidence)
  • STOP_AI_DEV_ORCHESTRATOR? NO
  • STOP_RALPH? NO

No migration or stop plan is given, because none is warranted by either audit's evidence, independently or combined.
